**Coverage:** 180 franchise-season observations: 30 in each of 2021–2026. MLB regular-season results only. Numerical inputs are bundled, with source URLs and historical private-archive fingerprints in `data/multiseason/provenance.json`. The verified 2025 CSV and analytical module are unchanged. Publisher captures and raw API responses remain private.

**Important exception:** a complete AP 2022 payroll table could not be verified. The 2022 season uses The Baseball Cube's published Opening Day payroll as an **alternate definition**. It is available as a single-season view but is excluded from the default AP multi-year cohort. Including it requires an explicit checkbox and produces a labeled mixed-basis sensitivity view. There is no claim that all 180 payrolls use identical accounting. No unavailable AP amount was estimated, inferred from player salaries or replaced silently.

**Performance and identities:** official [MLB Stats API standings](https://statsapi.mlb.com/api/v1/standings?leagueId=103,104&season=2026&standingsTypes=regularSeason) and [teams](https://statsapi.mlb.com/api/v1/teams?sportId=1&season=2026), with `season` set to each year. Each record has its exact standings URL. Stable MLB team IDs are used as franchise IDs; Cleveland Indians/Guardians share 114 and Oakland Athletics/Athletics share 133. Historical names stay on seasonal rows; franchise trends use the 2026 display name. League/division membership comes from that year's MLB response. A location change does not create a new franchise.

**Final records:** some teams have 161 rather than 162 decisions in 2021, 2024 and 2026. These are the official final records. Validation permits those documented counts, checks league-wide wins/losses and runs balance, and reconciles every row to its source. Wins are never padded to 162. Winning percentage is wins ÷ (wins + losses); annual regressions use actual wins, as requested, so the small exposure difference remains a limitation.

**Calculations:** nominal payroll per win = payroll ÷ wins. Each year's 30 teams receive a separate ordinary least-squares regression of wins against payroll in $M, with an intercept. Residual = actual wins − annual fitted wins. Pearson r, R², slope and annual cost-per-win ranks use the full season before UI filtering. There is no pooled regression across the 180 rows. The original 2025 model, executive claims and Colorado sensitivity are retained; other seasonal views provide their own Colorado sensitivity check.

**Consistency leaderboard:** franchises are ranked by the equal-weight arithmetic mean of their annual residuals in the selected cohort. The screen also shows the range, sum and number of positive-residual seasons. All franchises must have the same years represented. Aggregate cost per win uses total payroll ÷ total wins, not an average of annual ratios. These are descriptive summaries: repeated franchise observations are not treated as independent samples. No multi-year p-value, confidence interval, forecast validation or management-skill estimate is reported. Teams play one another, and within-season regressions also have independence limitations.

**Inflation option:** [BLS CPI-U, all urban consumers, US city average, all items, not seasonally adjusted, CUUR0000SA0](https://api.bls.gov/publicAPI/v2/timeseries/data/CUUR0000SA0?startyear=2021&endyear=2026). The archived response supplies **March** for every year. Adjusted payroll = nominal payroll × March 2026 CPI ÷ that season's March CPI. This is labeled **March 2026 USD**, not a 2026 annual average or baseball-salary inflation. All six March observations are published; no incomplete full-year CPI is projected. This transformation changes dollar comparisons, but not an individual year's correlation, R², ranks or residuals because it scales every team's payroll equally. Payroll per win can be displayed on the same real-dollar basis. Seasonal scatterplots and primary models retain nominal USD.

**Source comparison:** different publishers' Opening Day figures disagree under different accounting conventions. The private audit retains the complete 2025 AP/TBC comparison; this candidate retains only the 30 TBC payroll amounts used for 2022. Neither TBC comparison figures nor its page captures are distributed. The distinction between payroll definitions remains material; no source was substituted to address publication rights.

MLB/USA TODAY 40-man present-value payrolls and competitive-balance-tax payrolls are additional distinct measures. Tax payrolls generally use contract average annual values and benefits, not this AP roster snapshot. Spotrac active-roster, total and tax payrolls are also distinct products. None are substituted into the primary AP series. AP itself distinguishes its [2026 salary study](https://apnews.com/article/d11458cc331fffa46a30f346b5ca395a) from daily 40-man MLB payrolls. Publisher consistency reduces, but does not eliminate, cross-year accounting risk.

**Acquisition and verification limits:** AP 2026 and TBC pages were readable through the web retrieval tool while direct HTTP downloads returned 403; the captures are retained only in the private archive. Other original AP/MLB inputs were saved HTTP responses and are also not distributed here. BLS supplies the CPI observations. Source matching verifies faithful extraction, not confidential contracts or underlying club books. MLB records were not independently rebuilt from every game. No second complete AP 2022 table is claimed. Copying published numerical facts is assessed separately from protected expression and acquisition terms. This candidate is not a blanket rights clearance; see `PUBLICATION_RIGHTS.md`.

**Interpretation:** payroll efficiency does not measure profitability, operating cost, revenue or return on investment. Regression association is not causation. The models do not control for schedule, talent, injuries, player development or luck, and this study does not identify which of those caused a result. Changing payroll accounting can change ranks and fitted residuals; use the explicit alternate-year inclusion as a sensitivity view.
